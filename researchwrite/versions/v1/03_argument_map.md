# Argument map

## Scientific tension

- Known: land-based PV delivers low-carbon electricity but can occupy pre-existing cropland.
- Unknown: whether cropland-sited PV causes additional off-site cropland conversion beyond background trends.
- Why it matters: induced conversion can create carbon emissions outside the PV footprint and reduce the apparent climate benefit of PV expansion.

## Central research question

At county scale, how much attributable iLUC carbon emission is caused by cropland-sited PV, what share of PV gross avoided emissions does it offset, and when do annual iLUC emissions peak and cumulative PV benefits pay back this carbon debt?

## Central thesis

The thesis is a hypothesis, not a result: county-level land pressure from cropland-sited PV may be displaced across space, producing heterogeneous iLUC carbon debts and carbon-payback times.

## Supporting arguments

### Argument 1: causal land response
- Claim: cropland-sited PV may increase stable new cropland relative to a valid counterfactual.
- Evidence required: event-study pre-trends, treatment-intensity gradient, distance decay, non-cropland PV controls and placebo tests.
- Limitation: gross cropland overlap is exposure, not verified effective agricultural loss.

### Argument 2: territorial carbon consequence
- Claim: attributable new cropland can be converted into iLUC emissions using source-specific carbon-stock changes.
- Evidence required: prior land class, carbon pools, transition factors, horizon and uncertainty.
- Limitation: source and responsibility counties may differ.

### Argument 3: climate-benefit offset and payback
- Claim: cumulative iLUC emissions can be compared with cumulative PV gross avoided emissions.
- Evidence required: Phase/county generation, marginal grid EF, operating life and post-2022 scenarios.
- Limitation: this is not county-wide carbon neutrality.

## Counterarguments and alternatives

- Cropland expansion may be driven by food prices, land-balance policy, urbanization, climate or reclamation programs rather than PV.
- Classification noise, rotation and temporary fallow may mimic stable expansion.
- PV capacity and marginal EF measurement errors may dominate county offset ratios.
- Cross-county displacement may make territorial and responsibility rankings diverge.

## Final move

Estimate the causal land response first. Carbon accounting and payback are permitted only after the induced-area gate passes.
